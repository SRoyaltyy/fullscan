### Channel 2 — Utilities / XLU Environment, 2026-11-10

**1. Shared macro regime as it hits Utilities.** The dominant macro object today is the pending October CPI print (Tuesday 08:30 ET), with headline expected +0.3% m/m and core +0.2% m/m. Markets are pricing an 84% probability of a December Fed hike, up from ~74% a week ago after the strong payrolls print. The 10Y is pinned near 4.88% — two-month lows, but not falling — and the 2Y has edged up to 4.55%. For a bond-proxy, this is the critical asymmetry: a hot CPI validates the hawkish hike path and pushes the long end higher (bond-proxy selloff); an in-line-or-cool CPI removes the rates excuse but simultaneously invites risk-on rotation away from defensives (utilities lag). Neither branch is a clean positive. That two-sided negativity is precisely the setup the standing Utilities CPI lesson addresses: S0 should carry a negative score, not a neutral one .

The cross-asset tape is risk-on-leaning: ES +0.20%, NQ +0.41%, Asia composite +1.78% (Nikkei +2.87%, Kospi +4.39%), Europe +1.08%, VIX 20.04 with VIX/VIX3M at 0.96 (contango, no fear spike). Oil is down 1.59% (WTI 104.16) on US-Iran peace optimism. For a defensive bond-proxy, green futures and offered oil are headwinds, not cushions — they reduce the flight-to-safety bid that supported XLU's massive 1m relative outperformance (+9.07% vs SPY). VIX at 20 is not high enough to sustain a deep defensive bid; the lesson requires VIX ≥ ~20 *with* an explicit flight-to-quality impulse before a relative-beat claim is allowed, and the impulse is absent today .

**2. Sector spine factors.** The AI-power structural narrative is alive and has fresh, same-session catalysts. Constellation Energy (CEG) raised 2026 adjusted operating EPS guidance to $12.50–$12.80 versus the $12.17 FactSet estimate, citing expanded long-term nuclear deals with Amazon and Google . NRG Energy guided 2026 adjusted EPS to $7.90–$9.90, a wide range but with a midpoint above the $8.73 consensus, driven by Texas data-center demand . Duke Energy narrowed its 2026 guidance to $6.70–$6.80 and expects the high end, with 5–7% EPS growth through 2030 . Anthropic is reportedly buying $3.4 billion of natural-gas turbines directly from Caterpillar, a tangible AI-power capex commitment . Morgan Stanley estimates US data-center power demand will reach 97 GW by 2028 against a 70 GW supply gap .

This is a genuine positive for the Utilities spine, and it is not merely a multi-year narrative — there are fresh guidance raises today. However, the sector layer explicitly says data-center load growth can override *mild* rate moves, not a hawkish CPI repricing. With 84% December hike odds and CPI pending, the rate move is not reliably mild. The load-growth positive therefore offsets but does not erase the macro headwind.

**3. Secondary factors / rate cases.** Rate-case news is mixed but not systemic. Baltimore leaders are opposing BGE's $156 million rate increase ; Wisconsin regulators approved a smaller-than-requested increase for one utility . Avista reaffirmed guidance but narrowed its recurring EPS range . No broad adverse rate-case shock is visible. Nuclear/gas policy support remains a background positive (CEG deals, gas turbine demand), and grid capex is being pulled higher by data-center load (Entergy boosted transmission spending) . Net secondary read: modestly positive on load-growth capex, neutral-to-slightly-negative on rate-case affordability politics.

**4. Breadth / leadership inside the sector.** Breadth is the weak link. A November 2 scan showed “narrow leadership breadth” with 0/55 names above pivot and zero breakouts in the utilities screen . The XLU 1d relative return was −0.84% on 2026-11-09 even as SPY rallied +2.37% — the ETF participated but lagged badly on the day. The 3d relative (+2.25%) and 1w (+0.20%) are positive but stale; the freshest 1d print is negative and, per the standing lesson, the 1d relative tape dominates longer-horizon strength for a 1d call. The 1m relative (+9.07%) is enormous, but that is the cushion that was built during the AI-led risk-off; with futures green today and the AI sell-off not escalating in the premarket, that cushion is more mean-reversion fuel than fresh support .

**5. Flows / positioning.** XLU attracted roughly $2.57 billion in net inflows over the past month, and the sector has beaten the S&P 500 over the last 21 trading days . That is real demand, but it also creates crowding risk: the 1m relative outperformance is extreme, and the options market was pricing a wide expected move (41.09–46.26 by early December) . Flows are a two-sided input: supportive of the medium-term trend, but a source of vulnerability if the CPI print triggers a rate backup or a risk-on rotation.

**6. Earnings / guidance and policy catalysts.** The guidance raises from CEG, NRG, Duke, and Ameren are the freshest sector-level positives . The Anthropic-CAT turbine deal and the broader AI-power bottleneck narrative (Xinhua, Morgan Stanley) reinforce the structural case . On the policy side, midterm-election outcomes are raising questions about data-center regulation and utility affordability, a background risk rather than a same-day scored event . No utility-specific negative catalyst is visible for today.

This is a divergence: factors point down, tape confirmation is neutral. Per the shared method, trust factors over tape, but the flat premarket and the fresh load-growth catalysts argue against a notable-down call.
