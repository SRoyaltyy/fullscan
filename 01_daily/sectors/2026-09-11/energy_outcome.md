**The real interaction the morning missed:** *beta × barrel*. The morning treated S0 (risk-on tape) and S1 (offered barrel) as independent and roughly offsetting, netting to a mild down. In reality they *interacted*: a green CPI tape lifts the whole energy complex, and the offered barrel then determines *how much XLE lags* rather than *whether XLE falls*. The correct model is:

> XLE_absolute ≈ beta_lift (positive, large on CPI day) + barrel_drag (negative, moderate)

The morning modeled only the second term and applied it to the first quantity. That is the structural error.

1. **On a green CPI/risk-on tape, do not score S0 as a headwind for a commodity sector.** Beta is a *bid* for energy equities even when the barrel is offered. The correct expression of "offered barrel + green tape" is **up absolute / lag relative**, not **down absolute**. S0 sign must follow the equity tape, not the commodity.
2. **A −2.5% to −3.4% single-day crude pullback inside an +8% week, with the barrel closing above $100, is not a "collapse."** The morning's own prose said "not a collapse" while its hit-grid scored "Crude price collapse | HIT." Fix the hit-grid/prose inconsistency; S1 should be −0.5, not −1, in this configuration.
3. **Record product cracks (diesel $6) are a refiner tailwind, not a "crack spread collapse."** The morning scored the crack channel backwards. When products are at records, the refiner sleeve is a live positive contributor to XLE and must not be suppressed.
4. **The 09-10 crowded-long lesson works — but it predicts *relative* underperformance, not absolute down.** S3 = −0.5 was right and delivered −0.53% rel. Keep it, but stop letting it set the *sign* of the absolute call.
5. **Magnitude discipline saved the day.** The 09-08/09-09 cap and the 09-10 "do not emit notable" rule kept a wrong-sign call at mild. That is the system working as designed — a wrong call at mild is cheap.
