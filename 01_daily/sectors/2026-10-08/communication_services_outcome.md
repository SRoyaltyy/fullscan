## 2. Audit of the morning S0–S4 reads against reality

I am auditing the **morning numbers as written**, not rewriting them.

### S0_SHARED_MACRO: −1 → **MISS (wrong sign)**

The card's S0 rested on: hawkish FOMC minutes (10-07), 10Y 5.27% / 5.36% intraday high (highest since 2002), DFII10 2.91 (+48bp 1m), a 10Y auction supply amplifier, and a global long-end blowout (UK 30y gilts at 28-year high). All of that was **factually correct and confirmed by the tape** — the MarketWatch and AOL pieces both confirm yields took a toll broadly.

Where S0 failed was in **transmission, not in level**. The card itself flagged the critical nuance: *"5-day corr 10Y vs SPX: +0.303 — the duration channel is not the −0.96-class pipe of 09-25/09-28; it is a moderate, even positive, correlation."* The card then wrote that nuance down as a **magnitude cap** ("−1, not −2") when it was actually a **sign warning**. A +0.303 correlation between the 10Y and SPX means that on a rates-shock day, the *index* and the *rate* can move together — and more importantly, it means the rates channel is **not reliably negative for duration equities**. The card had the diagnostic in hand and used it to size the bet rather than to question it.

**Verdict on S0:** The macro facts were right; the sign inference was wrong. This is a **knowable-at-open** error — the +0.303 correlation was in the card's own Channel 1.

### S1_SECTOR_FACTORS: −1 → **MISS (wrong sign)**

S1 rested on two legs: (a) the fresh EU **CORE levy** (TechTimes, 10-07) hitting both anchors, and (b) the *absence* of fresh positive spine proof (ad/AI narrative carried, not fresh).

Leg (b) is a **structural error in the scoring method**, not a data error. The card explicitly reasoned: "no same-morning ad-spend print → no fresh positive → score negative." But **absence of fresh positive news is not a negative factor; it is a zero factor.** The card converted a null into a −1. That is precisely the failure mode the card's own memory section warned about in a different form — the 10-02 "all-zero-card-vs-engine-override" lesson, where a default zero got minted into a direction. Here the inverse happened: a null got minted into a negative.

Leg (a) — the CORE levy — was a real, dated, fresh negative. But it was a **tax/regulatory headline with no quantified earnings impact**, hitting two names, on a day when those names' price action (GOOGL +1.11% pre-market, and the sector closing green) showed the market did not trade it. A single unquantified regulatory headline does not deserve a full −1 on a 5-point scale when the tape is simultaneously rejecting it.

**Verdict on S1:** One real but unquantified negative (CORE levy) + one **methodological error** (null treated as negative). Net should have been **0**, not −1.

**The deeper error:** the card used a **pre-market ETF quote** to infer **breadth**. Breadth is a cash-session, constituent-level property. A −0.63% PM print on a two-name book tells you almost nothing about the other ~57% of the ETF. **Verdict on S2:** Wrong sign, and the error was **knowable at open** — the card had no constituent-level breadth evidence, only a PM quote, and it scored −1 on an inference it could not support.

## 3. Interactions / double-count / knowable-at-open test

**Double-count audit:** The card was actually *disciplined* here. It correctly collapsed FOMC minutes + 10Y auction + 10Y level into "one rates object" (S0), and eMarketer + Meta-overtakes-Google + Gemini 4 into "one carried ad/AI object" (S1). It did not stack S0 and S2 as one rates object. **No double-count error.** The problem was not over-counting negatives; it was **mis-signing** them.

**The real interaction the card missed:** The card treated the rates shock as a **tax on duration** (S0 negative) *and* treated the rotation into defensives/energy as evidence that XLC was "in the out-bucket" (S2 negative). But these two reads are in **tension**, and the card resolved the tension in the wrong direction. In a rates-shock tape where the market is *selecting* a narrow set of quality-growth names rather than liquidating growth broadly, the correct read is: **the rates shock is a rotation driver, not a de-rating driver.** The card's own Channel 1 showed the tell — XLE +1.85% and defensives bid *alongside* a rates shock is a "real-asset hedge" signature, which the card correctly identified for metals but failed to extend to the equity rotation logic.

**Knowable-at-open test — the decisive question:** Was the correct call (up/notable, or at minimum flat) knowable at the open?

**Partially yes, and the card had the evidence:**

1. **The +0.303 10Y/SPX correlation** was in the card's own Channel 1. A moderate-to-positive correlation is a **sign warning** for a rates-shock-short thesis. The card used it only to cap magnitude.

2. **GOOGL +1.11% pre-market** — one of two anchors, ~20-21% of the ETF, was *green* into a red tape. The card explicitly forbade this from minting an up call (09-09 lesson). That ban is correct *for a single name driving the whole call* — but the card then let META −0.86% and a −0.63% PM print drive a **down** call, which is the same error in mirror image. The 09-09 lesson cuts **both ways**: if GOOGL can't mint up, META can't mint down.

3. **The non-anchor sleeve had no negative evidence.** The card *inferred* drag from the PM print. It had no constituent prints for NFLX/DIS/T/VZ/TMUS/CMCSA. Scoring −1 on S2 breadth with **zero constituent-level breadth data** was not knowable — it was a guess dressed as a read.

4. **The PM print itself.** The card called −0.63% "a real print." But the card's own memory section contains the 09-16/09-18 lesson: *"do not convert NQ=F −0.67% / XLK −0.83% into an XLC call in either direction — use the XLC print itself, −0.63%."* The card followed the letter of that lesson (used the XLC print) but violated its spirit: it used a **pre-market** XLC print as if it were the cash print. The lesson's point was that the XLC *cash* print is the object; a PM quote is a different, thinner object.

**Conclusion:** The correct call was **knowable at open as "not down"** — the card had a positive-correlation rates channel, a green anchor, and no constituent-level negative evidence. Whether the correct call was "up/notable" vs "flat" is genuinely harder; the +1.15% relative win was not fully knowable. But **"down/mild" was not supportable**, and the card's own evidence contained the contradiction.

## 5. The core lesson

The morning card's fatal move was **treating a pre-market quote as the tape**. It built a coherent, well-argued, internally-consistent negative thesis on top of a −0.63% PM print, then used that thesis to *confirm* the PM print, then called the agreement "the cleanest agreement in this sector's recent memory." That is **circular**: the PM print was both the input and the confirmation.

The card's memory section is full of bans against letting a single name or a stale relative print drive the sector call. It has **no ban against letting a pre-market quote drive the sector call** — and that is the gap this session exposes. The 09-16/09-18 lesson ("use the XLC print itself") needs to be sharpened: **use the XLC *cash* print, and treat the PM quote as a thin, low-information object that cannot carry a −1 breadth score.**

Secondary lesson: **absence of fresh positive news is a zero, not a negative.** The card scored S1 negative partly because it found no same-morning ad/AI proof. That is a null. Nulls are zeros.

Tertiary lesson: **a moderate-to-positive rates/equity correlation is a sign warning, not just a magnitude cap.** The card had +0.303 in hand and used it to size the bet instead of to question it.
