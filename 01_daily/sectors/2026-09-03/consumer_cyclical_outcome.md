### 3. Interactions / double-count / knowable-at-open test

**Double-count check:** The morning correctly avoided double-counting oil (counted once in S1 level) and the Fed (counted once as carried). The failure was not double-counting — it was **under-counting a fresh catalyst**. Waller's comments were a *new* S0 driver that the morning had no visibility into because its Channel 1 futures snapshot (ES +0.13%) predated the speech.

**Knowable at open?** **No.** The morning's data window showed flat futures (ES +0.13%/NQ +0.16%), consistent with Waller's comments breaking **after** the snapshot. The Guardian live blog and Reuters coverage dated 09-03 suggest the speech/Q&A occurred during the trading day. A trader at the open would have seen the same flat futures the morning saw. The ISM Services beat (10:00 ET) was also after the open. The TSLA pre-market strength (~+1%) was visible but the morning correctly judged it insufficient to flip the call on its own — the +5–7% close was a same-day acceleration.

**Interaction:** Waller (rate relief) × ISM Services beat (activity strength) × TSLA Cybercab-day surge (single-name) compounded. The morning's "mixed regime / flat" read was reasonable given its data window but could not anticipate the dovish pivot.

### 5. Lessons for the scope
1. **"Carried" Fed overlays can go stale in one headline.** The morning's S0=0 rested on "Warsh/Fed already paid 09-01/09-02." A single dovish counter-signal (Waller) invalidated that in hours. When a Fed narrative is live and two-sided (hike vs hold odds near a coin flip), the S0=0 "carried" treatment is fragile — a fresh official comment is a legitimate S0 driver.
2. **Cybercab-type single-name events deserve a magnitude cap, not just a direction veto.** The morning correctly refused to flip direction on the livestream, but the *pre-event* run-up (TSLA +5–7% on the day) was a real, tradeable XLY driver. The rule should be: don't predict the *outcome* of the event, but DO weight the *anticipation* rally into the mega-cap weight.
3. **ISM Services beat was mis-scored as "activity, not XLY spend."** On a risk-on day, a strong services print is a broad equity tailwind that lifts the consumer complex regardless of its direct spend linkage. The taxonomy's "activity ≠ spend" distinction is correct for *fundamental* scoring but wrong for *tape* scoring on a macro-rally day.
