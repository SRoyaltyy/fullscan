## 3. Interactions / double-count / knowable-at-open test

**Double-count identified:** S2 (breadth laggard) and S4 (ETF tape confirmation) are the **same signal** — XLI's relative underperformance — measured over different windows (1m/1w vs 1d). The note explicitly claimed it was scoring the lag "once here (09-04: do not double-count in S4)" — but then scored S4 = −1 anyway, justified by the 09-09 "tape confirms" correction. The correction was used as a *license to double-count*. That is the mechanism of the miss.

**Interaction the note missed:** Oil's **sign flip within XLI**. The note modeled oil as a pure cost headwind. In reality, on a >$105 Brent day, XLI contains both losers (transports, airlines, trucking — fuel cost) and winners (energy-capex industrials, defense). These partially cancel. The note scored only the loser side. This is a **composition error**, not a data error — the data (oil up) was right; the mapping to XLI's P&L was one-sided.

**Knowable-at-open test:**
- Oil escalating: **knowable** (Brent already >$101 at the open, and the note knew it).
- Yields up / long-end pressure: **knowable** (30Y already selling off at the open).
- Defense bid on Iran escalation: **knowable** — the note *saw* the escalation and *chose* to score defense as MIXED. The Fool note confirms defense names jumped. This was a knowable positive the note deliberately zeroed.
- XLI's laggard status mean-reverting: **partially knowable** — a 1m rel of −5.56% with the broad tape selling off uniformly is a setup where dispersion compresses. The note had the data but drew the wrong inference.
- Copper fade not transmitting: **not knowable at open** — this is a genuine post-hoc observation.

**Net:** The direction was knowable and correctly called. The *magnitude* miss was substantially knowable-at-open — the note had every input needed to score S0 at −0.5 (oil sign) and to avoid the S2/S4 double-count. This was not an unpredictable session; it was a **mapping and scoring-discipline failure**, not an information failure.

## 5. Verdict

**Direction: HIT.** XLI fell −0.72%. The morning call was "down." That is correct.

**Magnitude: HIT on band, MISS on conviction.** The band was "mild," and −0.72% is mild. But the *relative* call — the thing that makes a sector prediction worth making — was wrong. XLI did not meaningfully lag; rel was −0.12%. A sector call that says "down/mild" on a day SPY is −0.60% is barely distinguishable from an index call. The note's own confidence was 0.55, appropriately modest, but the score of −2.7 implied more conviction than the outcome justified.

**The honest grade:** Direction correct, relative-return thesis **wrong**, magnitude band correct by luck of a broad-market down day rather than by sector-specific insight. The note got the macro right and the sector wrong, and the sector is the job.

**What to carry forward:**
1. **A confirming tape is not an independent factor.** S4 must not re-score what S2 already scored, even when the 09-09 correction says "don't flatten." The 09-09 correction is about *not vetoing* a directional call — it is not a license to *add* a confirmation point.
2. **On an oil-spike day, score oil's sign for the specific sector, not generically.** XLI is not a pure oil-cost loser. The note needs a composition check: what fraction of the sector's revenue is oil-sensitive-positive vs oil-cost-negative?
3. **A persistent laggard is not a same-day short.** 1m rel of −5.56% is a *condition*, not a *forecast*. In a uniform risk-off tape, dispersion compresses and laggards converge. The note should require a *fresh* catalyst to score the laggard as a forward factor.
4. **Geo escalation on an Iran-war day is a defense bid, not a neutral.** The 08-18 caution against manufacturing HITs was over-applied. When the escalation is *live and escalating* (Brent >$101 → >$108), defense is a knowable positive for XLI's composition.
