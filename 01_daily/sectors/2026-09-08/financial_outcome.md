## 1. What Drove the Sector Today

The dominant driver was the **live geopolitical/oil supply-shock risk-off overlay** — Iran war escalation pushing WTI +3.18% to $94.4 and Brent +2.16% to $99.1 (near $100). This was correctly identified in the morning prediction as the primary catalyst.

The transmission mechanism into financials was **broader than anticipated**. The morning analysis assumed financials would be "relatively shielded" as value/cyclical vs. long-duration tech. In reality, XLF fell **−1.38% vs SPY −0.55%** — a relative underperformance of **−0.83%**, meaning financials were NOT shielded; they were hit harder than the broad market.

Why did financials underperform on an oil-driven risk-off day?

1. **Oil spike → inflation expectations → long-end yields elevated** (30Y at 5.25%, 10Y at 4.77%). While the morning correctly identified this as a "bear/long-end steepener" (not NIM+), the magnitude of the yield move's impact on financials was underestimated. Rate-sensitive financials (mortgage REITs, regional banks with duration exposure) get squeezed when long-end yields spike on stagflation fears.

2. **Stagflation shock disproportionately hits cyclicals with credit sensitivity.** Financials are not just "value" — they carry credit books. An oil-driven inflation shock raises default risk expectations even if credit spreads haven't blown out yet (HY 2.65). The market was pricing forward credit deterioration.

3. **Capital markets / financial data names were weak** (MS/GS/SPGI/CME per MAP HEAT) — these are rate-sensitive and fee-sensitive; an oil shock that could force Fed hawkishness compresses valuation multiples on fee-based businesses.

4. **The gap-down open ($57.71 vs prior close $57.90) failed to find buyers** — the close at $57.30 shows sustained selling pressure throughout the session, not just an opening gap.

**Evidence**: Reuters market coverage (oil near $100, Iran war); Channel 1 tape (ES −0.44%, NQ −0.16%, DJIA −0.91%); XLF open $57.71 → close $57.30.
