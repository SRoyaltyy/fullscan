**Primary driver: Oil-driven risk-off with financials as an inflation/rate casualty, not a beneficiary.**

The 5-day 10Y-SPX correlation of **-0.948** (strongly negative) confirms that rising yields were hitting equities broadly.

However, the key transmission to financials was more specific than just "risk-off." The **long-end yield spike (30Y at 5.25%, 10Y at 4.77%)** in a stress-zone configuration is a double-edged sword for banks:

- **Negative channel:** Rising long-end yields signal inflation risk and potential Fed hawkishness, which compresses equity valuations broadly. For financials specifically, a bear steepener driven by term premium/inflation fears is NOT the NIM-positive steepener that helps banks — it's the 08-17 type that hurts rate-sensitive financials (mortgage REITs, consumer finance, long-duration assets within the sector).
- **Negative channel 2:** The oil shock raises recession odds, which directly threatens credit quality expectations. While HY spreads were still tight (2.65), the market was pricing forward deterioration risk.
- **Negative channel 3:** Capital markets and financial data names (MS, GS, SPGI, CME) were weak per MAP HEAT — these are the higher-beta financials that get sold first in risk-off tape.

**Secondary factor: Canada tariffs** added a second layer of uncertainty, though this was a smaller contributor than the oil shock.

**Notable:** The sector did NOT benefit from any "flight to quality within value" dynamic. Money centers (JPM/BAC) were relatively steady per MAP HEAT, but the breadth was not sufficient to hold the ETF up. The internal mix (money centers bid, capital markets/data weak) was not enough to offset the broad risk-off drag.
