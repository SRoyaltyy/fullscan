## 1. WHAT DROVE THE SECTOR

The taxonomy-aligned driver is unambiguous and it is the one the morning named: **a global duration/rates shock hitting a long-duration bond proxy.** The 10Y breached 5% (highest since 2007), the 30Y hit its highest since June 2007, and the whole curve backed up into a hawkish pre-FOMC print. For a sector whose cash flows are discounted at the long end, that is the correct primary lens, and it is the lens the morning used.

But the *magnitude* of the transmission was the story, and it was tiny. XLRE fell 12 bp. That is not what a 5% 10Y print "should" do to a bond proxy on paper. Three things explain the gap between the macro shock and the realized move:

**(a) The rate shock was already priced.** The morning itself flagged this — the hawkish path was "already printed and priced" (Warsh "work to do," Aug core CPI surprise, ~94% hike odds into tomorrow's FOMC). The 10Y had been grinding toward 5% for a week (1w +19 bp per the morning's own DGS10 table). A yield level that is the *culmination of a well-telegraphed move* does not produce a fresh, large repricing in rate-sensitive equities on the day it prints the round number. The morning correctly scored the FOMC path as 0 — but it did **not** fully carry that logic through to the *rate level itself*, which was equally well-telegraphed.

**(b) The relative bid was real and it was defensive.** On a day when SPY fell 46 bp, XLRE fell 12 bp. That is textbook defensive/rotation behavior: when the broad tape sells off on a macro shock, low-beta, domestic, cash-flow-visible sectors *outperform* even when their own discount-rate math is adverse. The +0.34% relative print is the signature of money rotating *into* the sector as a hiding place, not out of it. The morning saw the +0.31% 1d rel cushion and — correctly per its own 09-08 gate — refused to let it flip the sign. But it treated the cushion as a *magnitude cap* when in fact it was the *dominant same-day force*.

**(c) The sector's own idiosyncratic negatives were small and offset.** The morning's S1 stack (DC AI-slowdown weakness in EQIX/DLR, industrial softness in PLD/PSA, office stress) are all real but all small sleeves or slow-moving. None of them produced a same-day ETF-level impulse large enough to add to the macro drag. Meanwhile the two positive pockets the morning explicitly noted — retail (SPG/O/MAC) and healthcare (WELL/CTRE) — plus the single-name catalysts (O/KKR €528M JV, VICI dividend raise, CTRE deal flow, NHI $272M) provided a genuine offsetting bid. The morning barred these from "carrying the call" — correct for *direction* — but they were sufficient to *absorb* the macro drag and leave the ETF essentially flat.

**Primary driver:** live 10Y ≥5% duration shock (real, confirmed) transmitted weakly into a bond proxy that simultaneously received a defensive rotation bid — net effect: small absolute loss, relative outperformance.
