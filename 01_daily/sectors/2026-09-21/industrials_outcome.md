## 1. What drove the sector today

This is the single largest scoring error of the day: the card treated "not unanimous" as "not happening," when in fact the two legs that mattered (ES, NQ) were the two that carried.

**(b) Real yields falling — PARTIAL → effectively FIRED.** The morning card had DGS10 4.94 (−0.07), DFII10 2.61 (−0.07), and scored "real yields falling" PARTIAL (0.55) with the note "1d real-yield dip is relief, not a recession-scare bid; level still high." For XLI — a short-duration, capex-cycle, real-economy sector — a yield retreat is close to irrelevant. **This is the crux of the day: the same macro input that powered SPY did nothing for XLI.**

**(c) Oil down — FIRED, and correctly NOT mapped onto XLI as a tailwind.** WTI fell under $100 intraday (Eurasia), extending the morning's −1.59% Finviz print. The morning card's insistence — "do not treat oil-down as a full cyclical tailwind," "08-13: tanker/Hormuz is the stale leg" — was **vindicated**. Oil fell hard and XLI did not benefit. **(d) Sector rotation out of industrials — FIRED.** The morning card carried this at 0.60 with "1w rel −1.43% / 1m rel −5.99%; leftover, not a same-morning flow shock." Today's −1.44% rel is that rotation continuing in real time. **Net:** XLI's absolute flat was *not* a flat sector. It was a sector that got left behind by a rally it could not participate in, with a mechanical ex-div drag on top. The correct one-line driver is: **tech/AI-led risk-on + yield retreat, with XLI excluded by its factor profile and its own persistent relative-laggard condition.**

**No double-count errors found.** The card's discipline here was genuinely good.

**The real interaction the card missed:** *risk-on tape × sector-relative-laggard condition.* The card treated these as two independent zeros that sum to zero. In reality they **interact multiplicatively in relative space**: a risk-on tape *amplifies* the relative penalty for a laggard sector, because the laggard's beta to the rally is near zero while its beta to the index is 1.0 on the downside. XLI's +0.11% against SPY's +1.55% is the arithmetic of that interaction. The card's additive S0–S4 framework has no term for it.

**Knowable-at-open test:**

- **Knowable at open:** that the bounce was XLK-led (XLK PM +0.98% vs XLE −1.29% / XLP −0.65% / XLU −0.63%, XLI absent from the board). That XLI was a 1w/1m laggard. That only ~16% of industrials were above their 20-dma. That oil was offered. That yields were retreating. That XLI was ex-div.
- **Not knowable at open:** the *magnitude* of the SPY rally (+1.55% vs the +0.20% ES futures print). The futures board materially understated the session.

**Verdict: PARTIALLY knowable.** The *direction* of XLI (flat) was knowable and was called correctly. The *relative* outcome (−1.44%) was knowable at open from the XLK-led breadth + laggard condition + weak internals — all three were in the card's own prose. The card had every input needed to say "XLI will be flat while the index rallies" and did not say it.

## 5. Verdict and lessons

**The direction call was right. The magnitude call was right. The relative call was catastrophically absent.**

The morning card predicted **flat/flat** and got **flat/flat** on absolute terms. By the sector rubric's own scoring, that is a **HIT** — and it is the fourth consecutive flat/flat call in this regime (09-16 HIT, 09-17 mag HIT, 09-18 MISS, 09-21 HIT). The keep-flat discipline is working.

But the card spent its entire self-audit on the question "flat or down?" and never asked "flat *relative to what*?" On a day when SPY moved +1.55%, a flat XLI is a **−1.44% relative event** — the worst relative outcome in the recent sequence, worse than 09-18's +0.56% rel *win*. The card's framework scored the absolute correctly and the relative not at all.

**Three concrete lessons:**

1. **"Index rallies, my sector doesn't" is a signed signal, not a zero.** When the card observes that the bounce is XLK-led and XLI is absent from the PM board, that observation should carry a **negative relative weight**, not be filed as neutral prose. The card made this exact observation three times today (S0, S2, S4) and scored it zero each time.

2. **The four-index confirmation gate is a breadth test being used as a direction test.** ES +0.20% / NQ +0.41% green with RTY/DJIA flat means "narrow rally," not "no rally." The gate should be re-specified: *direction* from ES/NQ sign; *breadth* from all four. Today the gate suppressed a correct +1 S0 read.

3. **The ex-div handling was a clean win and should be templated.** The card identified the 27 bp mechanical drag, correctly excluded it from scoring, and correctly noted it does not explain the relative miss. That is exactly right and should be the standard treatment.

**On the 09-17/09-18 "flatten, not down" rule:** it was right again today — XLI did not go down. But the rule as written has no relative branch. The 09-18 reflection said "a late electrical/CAT squeeze vs soft SPY is not a license to lift direction." Today's mirror-image lesson is: **a soft XLI vs a strong SPY is not a license to call it flat-and-done.** The rule needs a relative-return clause before the next session in this regime.
