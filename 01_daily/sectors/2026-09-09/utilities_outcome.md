### 3. Interactions / Double-Count / Knowable-at-Open Test

**Interaction identified:** The morning correctly identified the two opposing forces (rates-rising vs. defensive bid) but **misjudged their relative strength**. The 08-18 lesson (risk-off + rising yields → relative beat / flat-to-negative absolute) was applied, but the actual outcome was **relative underperformance** — the defensive bid did not even deliver relative outperformance. Why? Because the defensive bid on 09-08 was already **priced in** — the +1.41% relative cushion was the market's expression of the oil-shock defensive trade. When the shock did not escalate further (no new strikes on 09-09), the bid faded and mean-reversion took over.

**Double-count check:** The morning counted the oil shock twice — once as a rates-rising negative (S1) and once as a risk-off defensive positive (S1 offset). This was appropriate in theory, but in practice the oil shock was **not escalating** — it was static at $100+. A static shock does not generate a fresh defensive bid; it only sustains the rates headwind.

**Knowable at open?** **Partially.** The 10-year auction was a known calendar event, and the yield level (4.8%+) was known. The key unknowable was whether the defensive bid would persist or fade. However, the **mean-reversion signal** (prior day +1.41% relative outperformance on a risk-off day) was knowable — and the 09-08 lesson should have been updated to include the possibility that a large cushion on a **non-escalating** shock would revert.

**Key miss:** The morning treated the 09-08 cushion as a "direction override toward flat." The correct read was: a large cushion following a **one-day** defensive spike (not a multi-day trend) on a **static** (not escalating) shock is a **mean-reversion signal**, not a support level.

### 5. Lessons for Future Sessions

1. **A one-day defensive spike on a static shock is mean-reversion fuel, not a support level.** The 09-08 cushion (+1.41%) was built on an oil shock that did **not** escalate on 09-09. When the shock plateaus, the defensive bid fades and the rates headwind (which the shock created) dominates.

2. **The 09-08 lesson needs a qualifier:** A 1d relative cushion ≥ +0.4% overrides toward flat **only when the shock is escalating or fresh**. If the shock is static (oil holding $100, no new strikes), the cushion is a **sell signal** — the market already expressed the defensive trade.

3. **10-year auctions at 20-month yield highs are downside risks for utilities, even when the auction "goes well."** The auction priced at 4.834%, below expectations — but the **level** was the problem, not the tail. At 4.85%+, every incremental bp of long-end yield is a direct hit to bond-proxy valuations.

4. **S4 (tape) is a lagging indicator after a one-day spike.** When S4 = +1 is driven by a single session's defensive bid (not a multi-day trend), it should be **downgraded** in weight, not treated as confirmation.

5. **The "opposing forces net to zero" framing fails when one force is structural and the other is episodic.** Rates-rising is structural (oil at $100, Fed hawkish); the defensive bid is episodic (one-day flight to safety). Structural forces win over a session.
