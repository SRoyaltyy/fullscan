## 3. Interactions / double-count / knowable-at-open test

**Double-count check:** The card explicitly scored the rates object once in S0, the mortgage/credit channel separately in S1 as household-credit transmission, and the confidence collapse as a survey object. **This was clean** — no same-shock double-count. The self-audit was honest here.

**Interaction the card missed:** The card treated the index-level AI bid as a *non-signal* ("08-27 pattern — index beta ≠ sector participation certificate"). That was correct as a *participation* read, but it missed the **second-order interaction**: a strong index tape *lifts the absolute floor* for a mega-cap-concentrated sector ETF even when the sector's own breadth is weak. XLY's AMZN/TSLA sleeve caught enough of the AI bid to keep the ETF green. The card's framework had no term for "index beta provides an absolute cushion while relative still bleeds." That is the missing interaction.

**Knowable-at-open test:** Was the outcome knowable at the open?
- The **relative** underperformance: **YES.** 10-of-11 nested industries down, 1m rel −7.28%, rates shock live — all knowable pre-open. The card got this right.
- The **absolute** flat-to-up print: **PARTIALLY.** The card knew futures were green (ES +0.28%, NQ +0.57%) and VIX was low/contango. It *discarded* the green futures as a positive signal ("same discarded-anchor split as 09-16–09-24"). That discard was the error. Green futures + low VIX + a mega-cap-concentrated ETF = a real absolute cushion. The card had the information and chose not to weight it.

**Verdict:** The relative call was knowable and correct. The absolute call was knowable-at-open and the card got it wrong by discarding its own green-futures signal.

## 5. Lessons

1. **Separate the relative call from the absolute call.** The card's factor reads were right on relative and wrong on absolute. A net-negative sector card on a green-futures, low-VIX, mega-cap-concentrated ETF should predict "**flat absolute / down relative**," not "down/mild." The 09-16 rule ("net-negative card + sub-gate 1d rel authorizes down but caps at mild") needs a companion rule: **when futures are green and VIX is low/contango, the absolute band should be flat, not down.**

2. **Do not discard green futures as a positive signal when the ETF is mega-cap-concentrated.** The card discarded ES +0.28% / NQ +0.57% as a "discarded-anchor split." For a sector ETF whose top two holdings are ~40%+ of the book and are themselves mega-cap AI-adjacent names, green NQ futures *are* a sector signal. The 08-27 ban ("do not map XLK PM leadership into XLY") is about *participation*, not about *absolute cushion*. Those are different objects.

3. **The 09-23 gate worked.** Scoring the 7-signal down-lean rather than netting to zero produced the correct *relative* call. Keep it.

4. **The 09-24 magnitude lesson held.** The card refused to mint "notable" from a 30 bp seam. The actual rel was −0.33% — mild. Correct.

5. **New lesson candidate:** When the dominant factor (rates shock) is *level-persistent* rather than *increment-escalating*, its transmission into a mega-cap-concentrated sector is a **relative grind, not an absolute break**. The card treated the rates shock as if it would produce an absolute down session; it produced a relative one. Re-gate the "rates shock → down" mapping on whether the shock is *escalating* (new hawkish increment) or *persistent* (same level, no new news). Today was persistent.
