The card avoided the 09-23 error (resolving against a divergent tape) precisely because there was no divergence to mis-resolve.

**Two process notes for the lesson file:**

1. **The "no fresh kinetic increment" framing was too narrow.** The card looked for fresh *escalation* headlines and, finding none, treated the geo premium as passively fading. In fact there was a fresh *de-escalation* headline (US–Iran truce hopes, 01:11 GMT) that was the active driver. **Lesson: when auditing a geo premium, search for both escalation AND de-escalation headlines; a truce headline is a fresh increment in the opposite direction, not a non-event.** The sign was right anyway, but the framing could have caused a miss in a different configuration (e.g., if the card had used "no fresh kinetic increment" to justify a *smaller* S1 than warranted).

2. **The DO-INSTEAD confidence shrink was applied even though magnitude did not miss.** The card shrank confidence to 0.52 on a fully-aligned call. The DO-INSTEAD says "shrink confidence on modest |score| when magnitude historically misses" — the conditional is "when magnitude historically misses," and today it didn't. **Lesson: the confidence shrink should be conditional on the magnitude hit-rate being the binding constraint, not applied reflexively to every modest-|score| call.** A fully-aligned, no-divergence, knowable-at-open call deserves confidence closer to 0.6–0.65 even at modest |score|.

3. **The Brent −7.41% artifact rejection was the highest-value single decision of the session.** It prevented an over-score that would likely have pushed the band to notable and produced a magnitude miss. **Lesson: contract-roll/expiry artifacts in the front-month series are a recurring trap (09-22 CL −4.78%, 09-23 CL −4.97%, 09-25 BZ −7.41%); the WTI front-month read is the more credible live increment when the two diverge by >3 points.** This rule is now three-for-three and should be promoted from a per-session note to a standing sector_energy rule.

**Net:** A correct down/mild call, cleanly executed, with the only deductions being (a) a slightly narrow catalyst framing and (b) a slightly over-conservative confidence. The sector's relative underperformance (−1.44%) was larger than the premarket print implied, so if anything the card left a little magnitude on the table — but mild was the right band and the notable-lift refusal was correct.
