Meanwhile the same-morning reversal checklist is positive — futures ≥ +0.3%, real yields easing, oil not spiking — and the prior session’s tape already absorbed the top-holding drop without the sector falling. The stale negative is over-weighted, causing a missed defensive flight-to-safety up day.
CURRENT_BEHAVIOR: Scoring S1 negative on a prior-session WMT-style earnings drop; keeping S0 neutral/negative on a prior-day Fed-minutes headline; treating positive futures as a reason to cap or underweight defensive sectors; leaving the narrative total and the deterministic pipeline total unreconciled (narrative flat/flat vs pipeline down/flat, with the scored output down/flat).
CORRECTED_BEHAVIOR: For a defensive sector, first check catalyst freshness. If the negative catalyst is ≥1 session old and the same-morning reversal checklist is positive, treat the negative as already reflected in the tape and do not keep it as a fresh S1 negative. On a recovery day after a yield-driven sell-off, staples and health care can lead even with positive futures, so positive futures do not automatically mean defensives underperform. Use prior-session evidence — e.g., XLP holding flat while WMT fell ~9% — as confirmation that the broad defensive bid is strong. Score S1 at 0 or positive, let S2/S4 confirm, reconcile the deterministic output with the narrative, and lean flat-to-up/mild instead of down/flat.
