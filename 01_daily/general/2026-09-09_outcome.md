# STEP 3 — TEST INTERACTIONS BETWEEN FACTORS

### AMPLIFICATION: Oil spike × Fed hike odds × Yields
The Iran tanker escalation pushed oil above $100. Higher oil feeds inflation expectations. Higher inflation expectations → higher Fed hike odds (Warsh already hawkish). Higher hike odds → higher Treasury yields (10Y at 52-week high). Higher yields → equity multiple compression. This is a **stagflation amplification loop** — each factor compounds the next. This is the dominant interaction of the day.

### AMPLIFICATION: Alphabet regulatory drop × Tech sector weakness
Alphabet's ~3% drop (regulatory scrutiny) compounded with Apple's sell-the-news event and general tech weakness to drag the NDX down 0.64%. Meta's 5-7% gain partially offset but couldn't overcome the combined drag.

### OFFSETTING / MASKING: Meta Muse gain vs. broad market decline
Meta's strong gain (from yesterday's Muse launch) masked some of the tech weakness. Without Meta, NDX would have been down closer to 1%. The market was weaker than the headline NDX number suggests.

### OFFSETTING / MASKING: Treasury buyback vs. yield pressure
The Treasury's tripling of buybacks to $6B was intended to support the long end, but yields still rose to 52-week highs. The buyback was too small to offset the fundamental drivers (inflation, supply, Fed). This is a case where a policy intervention was overwhelmed by market forces.

### CONDITIONAL / MODIFIER: Apple event under risk-off tape
Apple's foldable iPhone reveal would normally be a positive catalyst. But under a risk-off tape driven by oil and yields, the event became a "sell-the-news" opportunity. The same event in a risk-on environment would have had a different impact.

### SHARED-ROOT DOUBLE-COUNTING: Iran/oil/yields/Fed
The Iran tanker escalation, oil >$100, rising yields, and higher Fed hike odds are all **symptoms of one root cause**: the Hormuz supply shock. Counting each as a separate factor risks double-counting. However, each does have a distinct transmission channel (kinetic risk, commodity price, rates, monetary policy), so treating them as related but distinct is appropriate.
