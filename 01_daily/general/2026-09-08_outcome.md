## STEP 3 — INTERACTIONS BETWEEN FACTORS

### AMPLIFICATION: Oil spike × Fed hike expectations × High yields

The Houthi attack pushed oil toward $100, which feeds directly into inflation expectations. With CPI due Thursday, the oil spike will be captured in the print, reinforcing the narrative that the Fed (under hawkish Warsh) may need to hike at the September FOMC. Higher hike odds → higher yields → multiple compression on equities. This is a classic stagflation amplification loop: oil → inflation → Fed → yields → equities down. Each factor compounds the next.

### AMPLIFICATION: Amgen crash × Dow concentration

Amgen is a Dow component. A −10% move in Amgen translates to roughly −100 Dow points directly. Combined with Salesforce −4.5% (another Dow component), these two stocks alone account for a significant portion of the Dow's −628 point decline. The Dow's underperformance vs. the S&P and Nasdaq is largely explained by this single-stock concentration effect.

### OFFSETTING / MASKING: Energy stocks up vs. everything else down

While the broad market sold off, energy stocks (Exxon, Chevron, etc.) likely rose on the oil spike. This created a partial offset within the S&P 500 — energy is one of the largest sectors — masking the true breadth of the selloff in non-energy names. The S&P's −0.58% understates the weakness in tech, healthcare, and consumer names.

### CONDITIONAL / MODIFIER: AI disruption fears × High yields

The AI disruption narrative (Salesforce, ServiceNow down) is amplified by the high-yield environment. When discount rates are high, the market is less forgiving of companies with uncertain growth trajectories. Legacy software companies facing AI disruption have both earnings uncertainty AND high duration — a double whammy in a rising-rate environment.

### SHARED-ROOT DOUBLE-COUNTING: Oil/Houthi/Iran/CENTCOM as one cause

The Houthi attack, Iran tanker attack, CENTCOM strikes, and oil price surge are all symptoms of ONE root cause: the escalating US-Iran conflict. Counting each separately would overstate the number of independent drivers. However, each has distinct transmission channels (direct supply threat, shipping disruption, military escalation risk, inflation channel), so the multi-channel scoring is legitimate.
