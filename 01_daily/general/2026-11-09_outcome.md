## STEP 3 — TEST INTERACTIONS BETWEEN FACTORS

**AMPLIFICATION:** S7/S8 oil collapse + S4 dip-buying + S6 AMD upgrade + S12 short covering all pushed the same direction. The VIX crush (S11) then mechanically amplified the move as vol-control funds added exposure. This was a reflexive loop: lower oil → lower inflation fear → lower VIX → mechanical buying → higher equities → more short covering.

**OFFSETTING / MASKING:** Two large opposing forces were present, and the net move looks “calm” only in the sense that it was a steady trend — the day was anything but balanced.
- **Nvidia (S5) −5%** vs the broad rally (S4/S6/S8). The market sold Nvidia and bought almost everything else; the S&P still rose 2.3%.
- **Hawkish Fed (S2), December hike odds 84.5%** vs the rally. The market masked the hawkish Fed because oil collapse dominated the inflation narrative.
- **Energy sector** should have been hit by oil (S8), yet all 11 S&P sectors closed green — energy’s oil-driven weakness was masked by the broad risk-on bid.

**CONDITIONAL / MODIFIER:** The CPI print tomorrow (S9) is a conditional modifier on the entire rally. Today’s move was partly a bet that CPI will not surprise hot. A hot print would reverse the oil-driven inflation-relief trade. Similarly, the Hormuz report (S7) is unconfirmed — if denied, the oil collapse could snap back.

**SHARED-ROOT DOUBLE-COUNTING:** Oil collapse, energy-sector weakness, airline/transport gains, and breakeven inflation decline all trace back to **one cause**: Iran de-escalation hopes (S7). That is one cause with multiple symptoms, not four independent causes. The AI-regulation fear (S3) and Nvidia investigation (S5) are separate AI-related factors and should not be collapsed into one.

## STEP 4 — THE 9AM FORESEEABILITY TEST

**Yes, the dominant driver was knowable at 9:00 AM ET.**
- Trump’s weekend Iran comments were public before the open.
- Oil was already sharply lower premarket.
- Nvidia’s CEO investigation and the Aletheia downgrade were premarket news.
- The AMD Oppenheimer upgrade was published at 08:57 ET.
- Fed hike odds were known.

What was **not** fully foreseeable at 9am was the **magnitude and breadth** of the rally — specifically, the violent short covering in semis ex-Nvidia (SOX +6.6%) and the fact that all 11 sectors would close green. The direction was foreseeable; the amplitude was underestimated.

## STEP 5 — HUNT FOR WHAT DOESN’T FIT

- **Nvidia −5% while the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index rose 6.6%.** This was Nvidia’s largest underperformance versus the SOX since January 2019. The market was selling Nvidia specifically while buying the rest of semis.
- **Energy sector closed higher even as Brent fell 5.8%.** All 11 S&P sectors were green, which is unusual when crude is collapsing. The broad risk-on bid overpowered the oil-price headwind for energy equities.
- **10Y yield rose ~1bp to 4.87% while stocks surged.** This was not a flight-to-quality bond rally; it was a good-news-is-good-news regime where higher yields did not impede equities.
- **Bitcoin was weak (−0.53% premarket) while risk assets surged.** Crypto did not confirm the risk-on move.
- **Russell 2000 +3.1% outperformed the S&P 500.** Small caps joined aggressively, indicating breadth expansion rather than a narrow mega-cap bounce.
