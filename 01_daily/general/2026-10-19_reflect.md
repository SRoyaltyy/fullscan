The standing lesson was applied in its literal form (spine over tick) and that application **hurt** the mechanism read. This is not a retrieval failure — the lesson was retrieved and applied; it was applied in a regime where the spine and the session diverged. That is a lesson-scope problem, not a checklist-enforcement problem.

**CHECK 2 — BACKWARD TEST.** Would "when the 1-week yield spine and the same-session 1-day tick point in opposite directions, do not score B2 positive on the spine alone" have helped on recent days? On 10-16 (yields down, stocks down, AI-credit idiosyncratic) the spine was the correct read and a same-day-tick override would have **hurt**. I will **narrow** it: only fire when the session's 1-day yield move is *observable pre-close* and *contradicts* the spine, and only to **cap B2 at 0**, not to flip it negative.

**CHECK 3 — CONFLICT SCAN.** Direct conflict with the standing 10-16 lesson ("do not let a 1-day FRED tick override a 1-week yield/regime signal"). Resolution: the 10-16 lesson governs **premarket scoring** (do not promote a 1-day print as a new signal at 9AM). The proposed narrowing governs **post-hoc mechanism attribution** (when the session's realized yield direction contradicts the spine, do not credit B2 as a positive *driver* in the autopsy). These are different layers — premarket scoring vs postmarket attribution — and do not overlap once stated that way. I will phrase the corrected behavior to apply **only to the postmarket attribution layer**, not to premarket B2 scoring.

**CHECK 4 — APPLIED-LESSON REVIEW.** The 10-16 standing lesson ("do not emit UP from leftover Warsh/yield relief against a live AI-credit tape") was **applied** — the prediction explicitly refused to restack Warsh into B1 and refused to score the 1m 10Y +10 bp as a fresh tightening.
