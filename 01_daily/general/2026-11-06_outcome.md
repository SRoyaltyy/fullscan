**3. SHARED-ROOT DOUBLE-COUNTING:**
- **Democratic sweep → AI regulation fear → semis selloff → credit spread widening → retail de-risking** is **one cause with multiple symptoms**, not five independent causes. The market was repricing a policy regime, and the flow damage was the transmission mechanism.

- **VIX backwardation (F11)** changes the meaning of the rebound. In a normal term structure, a +1% Nasdaq bounce would be read as improving risk appetite. In backwardation, the same bounce is more likely a **dead-cat bounce** — a chance to sell, not a durable turn. The PATH confirms that interpretation.

### STEP 5 — HUNT FOR WHAT DOESN’T FIT

**1. Dow −0.46% vs. NDX +1.09%.** The single largest divergence of the day. This was not a broad rebound; it was a **narrow tech/semis bounce** alongside continued selling in old-economy and consumer names (Nike, Walmart, McDonald’s, Home Depot). Breadth was poor beneath the index level.

**2. 10-year yield rose 6bps to 4.9% while stocks rebounded.** Normally, an equity rebound coincides with easing yields or stable yields. Here, **bonds sold off as stocks bounced** — a hawkish-repricing day, not a risk-on day. The bond market was telling a different story from the Nasdaq.

**3. Oil eased despite live tanker-attack headlines.** Geopolitical risk did not command a sustained premium on Nov 6. That is an outlier against the structural Iran conflict narrative and suggests the market treated the headlines as noise, or that demand concerns and oversupply expectations dominated.

**4. VIX fell 2.67 points to 25.51 despite the intraday reversal.** If the close-near-low were a genuine new shock, VIX should have risen. The fact that it fell suggests options markets read the day as **relief that Thursday’s crash did not continue**, even though the intraday tape was weak. That is a meaningful divergence between equity price action and volatility pricing.

**5. Gold fell 0.94% despite elevated VIX and backwardation.** A stronger dollar and higher real yields likely dominated gold’s safe-haven bid. This is consistent with the hawkish-repricing story and inconsistent with a pure fear-driven market.
