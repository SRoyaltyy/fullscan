## STEP 3 — TEST INTERACTIONS BETWEEN FACTORS

### AMPLIFICATION
- **Democratic sweep × oil crash × bond rally.** These three factors pushed in the **same direction for equities**: the sweep removed political uncertainty and raised war-de-escalation odds → oil crashed → inflation expectations eased → bonds rallied → equities re-rated higher. The Dow’s trend-up path (closed at day high) reflects this compounding.
- **AMD beat × Apple reversal × buyback bid.** The tech/AI complex stabilised after AMD’s clean beat, and Apple’s intraday reversal added a second leg of mega-cap support, while the buyback window provided a structural bid. This is why NDX recovered from its morning dip, though it remained choppy.

### OFFSETTING / MASKING
- **Energy sector -4.4% vs. broad market +1.6%.** The S&P 500’s headline gain **masks a violent sector rotation**: energy was the worst-performing sector by far, down ~4.4–4.7%, while consumer discretionary led with +4.27%. The net index move looks “calm-ish” (+1.6%), but the **internal dispersion was extreme**. This is a textbook offsetting pattern: oil crash hurt energy but helped transports, consumer, and the broad market.
- **Apple’s morning drop offset by afternoon reversal.** Apple was a drag at the open, then a tailwind into the close. The net index effect is muted, but the **intraday narrative** is one of a sentiment flip.

### CONDITIONAL / MODIFIER
- **Oil crash is only positive for equities because it is supply-driven / war-de-escalation-driven, not demand-driven.** If oil had crashed because of a global demand shock, the equity reaction would have been negative. The **conditional modifier** is the midterm result: it changed the *meaning* of the oil move from “demand fear” to “peace dividend.”
- **Fed hawkish hold is a known headwind, not a fresh shock.** The November 3 hold was fully priced. Today’s rally occurred **despite** the hawkish Fed because the political/oil factors dominated. If the Fed had surprised with a hike, the interaction would have been offsetting; as it was, the Fed was background noise.

### SHARED-ROOT DOUBLE-COUNTING
- **Oil crash, energy stock decline, bond rally, and dollar weakness all trace back to ONE root cause: the Democratic midterm sweep and the resulting shift in Iran war expectations.** These are not four independent causes — they are **one political event with four symptoms**. The morning prediction correctly avoided double-counting the Hormuz headline as both an oil negative and a geopolitical negative; today’s review must similarly avoid counting the oil crash, energy sell-off, and bond rally as separate “drivers.”

## STEP 5 — HUNT FOR WHAT DOESN’T FIT

1. **Energy sector -4.4% while the broad market rallied +1.6%.** This is the biggest outlier. In a “risk-on” day, energy was the worst sector because the oil crash was a **sector-specific negative** even as it was a **macro positive**. The market did not treat lower oil as unambiguously good — it punished the producers.
2. **NDX lagged the Dow by ~1.6 percentage points.** Tech did not lead the rally. The Nasdaq 100 was choppy and closed well below its high (+2.67% intraday vs. +1.44% close), while the Dow trended up all session. This challenges the simple “AI/tech-led risk-on” narrative.
3. **Apple reversed from -4% to +6%.** A pure sentiment flip on a price-target change (Bernstein $370→$380) rather than a fundamental revision. This is a positioning/flow event, not an earnings event.
4. **Palantir fell ~5% despite a beat-and-raise.** The market penalised “noisy” bookings and valuation, showing that not all AI earnings beats are rewarded equally.
5. **Hormuz mine claims were ignored.** A headline that would normally spike oil (four tankers destroyed) was completely overridden by the midterm/oil-de-escalation narrative. This is a powerful signal that **political regime change dominated geopolitical risk** today.
6. **Bonds rallied (yields -6bp) while the Dow surged 3%.** In a “good-news-good” regime, you might expect yields to rise with equities. Instead, yields fell — consistent with the **oil crash lowering inflation expectations** and the **gridlock reducing fiscal supply fears**. This is a cross-asset divergence that reinforces the “peace dividend + gridlock” interpretation.
