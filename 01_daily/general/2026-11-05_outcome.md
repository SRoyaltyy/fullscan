## STEP 1 — ENUMERATE FACTORS ACROSS ALL FOUR DEPTH LAYERS

### STRUCTURAL / BACKGROUND (weeks-to-months old, still live)
- **AI capex super-cycle and its valuation edifice.** The market’s dominant secular theme in 2026 has been the buildout of AI data centres. By early November, that trade was crowded, institutional positioning was heavy, and year-end profit-taking incentives were building.
- **Democratic midterm sweep (Nov 3–4).** Democrats won both chambers, removing the tail risk of a contested outcome. Initially read as a risk-positive (the prior session rallied sharply), but it introduced a new structural overhang: expectations of tighter AI and data-centre regulation.
- **Fed regime: hawkish hold with a hiking bias.** The Fed left rates in the 3.75–4.00% range last week, but inflation remains above target and multiple officials have dissented in favour of hikes. The market broadly expects another increase in December.
- **Oil’s post-geopolitical collapse.** Crude had already fallen sharply on Iran diplomacy hopes before 5 November, removing the inflation-shock premium but also signalling demand fear and forcing energy-sector repricing.

### SETUP CONDITIONS (days-to-weeks old)
- **Semiconductor bear market already in progress.** The chip complex had peaked in June and was already selling off; Wednesday’s session saw a semiconductor rout alongside a falling VIX, an unusual and fragile configuration.
- **Post-midterm euphoria as a setup for reversal.** The prior session’s SPX +1.58% / Dow +3.03% was a political relief rally. T+1 digestion was always likely; the question was whether it would be orderly.
- **Elevated event uncertainty but low realised volatility.** VIX was in the high-teens premarket, and the options market was not pricing a tail event. This low-vol setup made the subsequent spike more violent.

### SAME-DAY TRIGGERS (5 November)
- **Qualcomm (QCOM) weak forward guidance.** QCOM fell ~10–11% premarket and closed down ~11% after issuing light Q1 guidance, citing component and manufacturing cost inflation. Handset chip sales fell 26% y/y.
- **Democratic data-centre regulatory overhang crystallises.** Reports and analyst commentary linked the Democratic sweep to possible moratoriums on data-centre construction, directly threatening the AI capex thesis.
- **Hawkish Fed speakers (Hammack, Barkin).** Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack said policy “will need to move to a restrictive stance” and favoured more frequent, smaller hikes. Richmond Fed President Thomas Barkin said inflation is “too high” and “gradual makes sense” for further hikes.
- **No fresh positive catalyst to offset the negatives.** The midterm tail risk was already paid; oil’s decline was a continuation, not a new shock; and the dollar was flat premarket.

### MECHANICAL / FLOW
- **Year-end de-risking and profit-taking.** Institutional investors were locking in gains in AI winners before year-end, and the selloff had “a lot of hallmarks of being pretty technical in nature,” per Horizon’s CIO.
- **Hedge-fund liquidation / forced selling.** Traders described the tape as a “liquidation” and “panic selling due to position liquidation by specific hedge funds,” amplified by crowded AI positioning.
- **Volatility-control and trend-following flows.** The VIX spike above 28 likely triggered mechanical de-risking from vol-targeting strategies, accelerating the afternoon trend lower.

## STEP 3 — TEST INTERACTIONS BETWEEN FACTORS

### AMPLIFICATION
**Qualcomm guidance + Democratic data-centre regulatory fears + hawkish Fed speakers** pushed in the same direction and compounded. QCOM provided the earnings catalyst; the Democratic sweep provided the *policy narrative* that the AI capex cycle could be structurally impaired; and Hammack/Barkin provided the *rates* headwind for long-duration growth stocks. Each alone would have been manageable. Together, they created a coherent “sell the AI trade” story that fed on itself.

### OFFSETTING / MASKING
**Treasury rally vs. equity crash.** Falling yields are normally a tailwind for equities, but on 5 November the bond rally was a *flight to safety*, not a growth signal. The offset was largely masked: the S&P 500 still fell 4.4% despite the 10Y yield dropping to ~4.83%. The bond market was telling a growth-scare story while the equity market was telling an AI-de-rating story — related, but not identical.

**Defensive sector resilience vs. tech carnage.** Defensives (consumer staples, utilities, health care) held up far better than technology, masking the true breadth of the damage. The equal-weight S&P 500 likely fell less than the cap-weighted index, but the headline number still looked catastrophic because of the mega-cap tech weighting.

### CONDITIONAL / MODIFIER
**The Democratic sweep changed the *meaning* of the AI capex trade.** Before the election, AI capex was a bipartisan growth theme. After the Democratic sweep, it became a *regulatory target*. The same event (Democratic victory) was positive for the broad market on 4 November (relief rally) and negative for AI/data-centre names on 5 November (regulatory overhang). This conditional shift is the single most important interaction of the day.

### SHARED-ROOT DOUBLE-COUNTING
**AI capex sustainability fears, data-centre regulation, semiconductor guidance, and Nasdaq weakness** are not four independent causes. They are **one root cause with four symptoms**: the market repricing the entire AI infrastructure complex under a new political regime. Qualcomm’s guidance was the *trigger*; the Democratic sweep was the *narrative frame*; the Fed was the *rates backdrop*. Treating them as separate causes overstates the number of independent shocks.

## STEP 5 — HUNT FOR WHAT DOESN’T FIT

- **Energy sector divergence.** Some sector data shows XLE *up* ~1.1% on the day, while other sources show energy equities falling sharply. This reflects the intraday whipsaw in crude: oil plunged ~8% at settlement, but energy stocks may have been supported by the geopolitical risk premium or by rotation into value. The divergence between the commodity and the equity is a genuine outlier.
- **Financials (XLF) underperformance.** In a classic risk-off session, financials often fall with the broad market. Some data shows XLF down ~1.6%, while defensives held up. This is consistent with a growth scare rather than a pure liquidity crisis.
- **Bonds and equities telling different stories.** Equities priced an AI-specific de-rating and a growth scare; bonds priced a flight to safety and lower inflation expectations. The two markets were not in simple “risk-on/risk-off” alignment — the bond rally was a *response* to the equity crash, not a shared macro signal.
- **Gold and silver surging.** Gold rose >3% and silver >5%, a safe-haven bid that often accompanies real-rate uncertainty. This fits the risk-off narrative but contrasts with the “calm” premarket dollar.
