## Narrative

**US stocks ended mixed after a volatile session as new Trump tariff proposals and hotter core PCE data pressured tech while cyclicals outperformed**

- The Dow Jones Industrial Average ($DJI) rose 0.33% to 50,182.95 after erasing an intraday loss of about 1,105 points, while the S&P 500 ($SPX) slipped 0.25% to 7,182.78 and the Nasdaq Composite ($COMP) fell 0.62% to 23,585.80 after rebounding from an early decline of roughly 4.6%.
- The Trump administration unveiled proposals for new 20% tariffs targeting China, the EU, India and other trading partners, slated to take effect no earlier than January, helping drive the early equity selloff.
- October core PCE inflation printed at 3.3% year-over-year versus 3.2% consensus and third-quarter GDP growth was sharply revised higher, reinforcing inflation worries and rate concerns.
- Seven of 11 S&P 500 sectors advanced, led by Materials with a 1.83% gain and strength in Industrials, while Technology lagged with about a 1% decline; the Russell 2000 (IWM) rose 0.71% to 2,704.06.
- HP Inc. (HPQ) jumped 11.5% after reporting Q4 adjusted EPS of $0.83 versus $0.75 expected on $16.0 billion in revenue, up 9.3% year-over-year, and issuing fiscal 2027 EPS guidance of $3.15–$3.45.
- Nvidia (NVDA) fell 3.9% to $149.30 after S&P placed it on negative CreditWatch with a possible cut from AA to AA-, and Lam Research (LRCX) dropped about 5.7% as semiconductor weakness persisted amid AI selling and export-control fears, though later-session dip buying supported some AI-related names, including a 3.4% gain in Meta Platforms (META).
- Nutanix (NTNX) slid 15.2% after announcing CEO succession alongside softer bookings despite an earnings beat, extending weakness in select software and infrastructure names.
- In after-hours trading, Palo Alto Networks (PANW) declined about 3.7% after a former executive was sued over allegations of premature revenue booking, which the company called meritless, while Banco Macro (BMA) also moved on results amid otherwise limited major earnings reactions.
- Deere & Co. (DE) rose about 4.4% after saying the large-ag cycle troughed in 2026 and guiding FY27 net income to $4.5 billion–$5.25 billion, with a holiday-shortened Friday session ahead and US markets closed Thursday for Thanksgiving.
