## RULE
Before finalizing a down call on XLC, run a reversal checklist: (1) is oil actually falling, not just below a headline level? (2) is there official supply-route/export data contradicting closure or severe disruption? (3) is the scheduled inflation print soft/in-line and already published? (4) are futures positive and XLC’s relative tape improving rather than lagging? If reversal signals are present, do not mechanically apply the prior “positive futures are only offsets” lesson; neutralize or flip the sector call, avoid double-counting the stale risk-off in S0-S4, and scan top XLC holdings for fresh filings/stake disclosures that were knowable at the open.

## WHEN IT FIRES
A prior-session geopolitical/oil supply-shock (e.g., Hormuz closure, Brent near $90) is still treated as “live” on an XLC call even though the pre-open package already contains reversal signals: oil is falling, an official source reports supply routes/export volumes near normal, scheduled inflation data is in hand/benign, futures are positive, and/or a fresh knowable catalyst exists in a top XLC holding. The model double-counts the stale risk-off across S0/S2/S3/S4, mechanically applies the older “positive futures are only offsets” rule, and emits down/notable. Actual outcome is a risk-on reversal in which XLC outperforms SPY by >1%.

## WRONG IF
If a future XLC session has all the reversal signals present — oil falling, Hormuz exports reported near normal, benign inflation, positive futures, and a fresh positive top-holding catalyst — yet XLC still closes down or materially underperforms SPY, then the reversal checklist is too weak and must also weight unresolved company-specific negatives (antitrust, Alphabet/Meta FCF, etc.) before relaxing the old cap.

## EVIDENCE
2026-08-13: predicted XLC down/notable (-9.0); actual XLC +2.07% vs SPY +0.70%, rel +1.37. Drivers: soft PPI/CPI fueled rate-cut hopes and a record-high SPX; oil fell after the US said Hormuz crude exports were near 9M bpd; NFLX rose ~3.4-5% after Ackman disclosed a stake. All five component scores were -1 and all were wrong on sign. The morning read’s own evidence (positive futures, prior-day CPI in hand, already weakening oil premise) contradicted the stale risk-off call.
