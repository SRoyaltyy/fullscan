## RULE
(1) Treat a record-close sequence (e.g. 12th record close since end-July) plus 1m rel ≥ +8% as a live crowded-long condition on its own — do not gate the crowding trigger on 1w rel >+5%. (2) Apply a marginal-increment asymmetry test: when the sector's own factor moves only sub-1.5% d/d while the sector is at records, net S1 toward 0/+1 rather than +2, because the marginal equity upside is small and the profit-taking downside at records is large. (3) Do not score S2 and S4 both positive off the same prior-close Channel 1 series — cap the combined breadth+tape contribution at +1. (4) Score S3 negative (crowded-long unwind risk) when a record-high sector with a multi-week run and a flow hangover faces only a small incremental factor move. (5) The pipeline-computed decision must honor the prose magnitude cap — emitting "notable" against a prose "mild" is a scoring-integrity failure.

## WHEN IT FIRES
A sector ETF sits at/near all-time highs after a multi-week relative run (1m rel strongly positive, e.g. ≥ +8%) with a sequence of record closes, while the sector's own fundamental driver (crude/geopolitical premium) is still green but its *incremental* move is small (sub-1.5% d/d). The model scores the sector's own factor cluster as a positive spine (S1 positive), scores prior-close Channel 1 tape as independent breadth/tape positives (S2 and S4 both positive off the same series), and nets positioning/flows to zero — producing an up call into a crowded-long unwind. The correct treatment is: record-close sequence + multi-week run IS the crowding signal (do not require 1w rel >+5%); small marginal factor increment against extended positioning is asymmetric DOWN; S2 and S4 must not both be scored positive off the same prior-close Channel 1 series.

## WRONG IF
If, on a future session with XLE at a record-close sequence (≥10 record closes in ~6 weeks) and 1m rel ≥ +8%, the sector's own factor (crude) rises ≥ +2% d/d with no fade and XLE closes green and outperforms SPY by ≥ +1%, then the record-close-sequence crowding trigger is falsified as a fade signal and should be demoted to a magnitude-only dampener. Conversely, if a record-close-sequence sector with a sub-1.5% marginal factor increment closes green and outperforms on ≥3 consecutive such sessions, the asymmetry test is falsified.
