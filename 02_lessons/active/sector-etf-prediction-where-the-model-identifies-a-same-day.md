## RULE
When a high-impact macro print is pending at the open and identified as two-sided, the model must widen the magnitude band to at least mild (not flat) regardless of component scores, because the unresolved binary is itself a source of variance that flat cannot capture. Additionally, when the model identifies a same-day macro catalyst as the dominant session driver, it should treat the sector's prior relative performance as context for direction but not as a magnitude cap — a broad-market rip on a macro beat can produce +1% absolute moves in sectors with no fresh catalyst, and flat predictions on such mornings should be reserved for sessions where no high-impact print is pending.

## WHEN IT FIRES
Sector ETF prediction where the model identifies a same-day two-sided macro catalyst (ISM Services) and refuses to pre-score it, futures are flat (ES +0.13% / NQ +0.16%), and the sector has no fresh same-morning catalyst — the model scores all factors zero and predicts flat/flat, but the actual session delivers a broad-market rip (+1.05% SPY) on the macro catalyst beat, producing a large absolute move in the sector ETF that the flat band misses entirely.

## WRONG IF
A session where a high-impact macro print is pending at the open, the model predicts flat/flat, the print comes in as expected (not a beat/miss), and the sector moves <0.5% — this would show that pending macro does not always require a wider band. However, the asymmetric risk (a beat or miss can produce ±1% moves while an in-line print produces small moves) means the expected variance is still higher than a no-print session, so widening to mild remains the safer default. A second falsifier: a session where the model widens to mild on pending macro, the print is in-line, and the sector moves <0.3% — this would show the widening was unnecessary, but it would be a cost of insurance, not a model error.

## EVIDENCE
2026-09-03 Industrials: predicted flat/flat (total_score 0.0, confidence 0.52) with ISM Services explicitly identified as "two-sided, not scored until print" and "do not pre-score a miss or a beat." Actual: XLI +1.03% absolute, SPY +1.05%, ISM Services beat drove a broad-market rip. The model's own text noted "ISM Services is not the manufacturing spine" and "Encode as event risk in S0" — but encoding as event risk with S0=0 meant the event contributed nothing to the score, and the flat band had no mechanism to capture the macro-driven upside. The 09-03 sector lessons for Basic Materials, Communication Services, Consumer Cyclical, Consumer Defensive, Energy, Financial, and Healthcare all document the same failure mode across sectors on the same day.
