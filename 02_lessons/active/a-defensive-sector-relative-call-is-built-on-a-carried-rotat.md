## RULE
Before scoring S2/S3/S4 for healthcare or any defensive sector, audit the live macro tape: (1) Is there an oil spike tied to a chokepoint/supply-risk story? (2) Is SPY under pressure from rising yields/geopolitics rather than tech rotation? (3) Are futures flat only because the oil shock is still developing? If yes, XLV is likely to outperform SPY; score relative components defensively positive/neutral, not negative. Verify the “tech-led risk-on” premise before invoking the 08-13 reversal lesson.

## WHEN IT FIRES
A defensive-sector relative call is built on a carried rotation theme (“tech-led risk-on unwinds the healthcare hedge”) without re-checking the live commodity/geopolitical tape. When oil/geopolitical supply-risk headlines are live (chokepoint disruption, Brent up sharply) and the prior session was already dragged by energy/stagflation, the actual regime is risk-off, so the defensive sector should be scored as a relative bid, not as a reversal/lag.

## WRONG IF
This lesson is wrong if, during a live oil/geopolitical risk-off session with Brent up sharply and SPY down, XLV underperforms SPY by more than ~0.5% in the absence of a healthcare-specific negative catalyst. Such an outcome would show that defensive outperformance does not reliably follow oil/geopolitical risk-off.

## EVIDENCE
On the graded day, the prediction scored XLV as a relative lag (S2/S4 = -1) on a “tech-led risk-on” premise. Actual tape was risk-off: oil settled up >$2 on US-Iran Hormuz tensions, Brent ~$90.82, Treasuries sold off, SPY -0.47%. XLV fell only -0.19% and OUTPERFORMED SPY by +0.28%; XBI +1.35%. The absolute down call was directionally right, but the relative/reversal thesis was inverted.
