## RULE
Before scoring S4=−1 on prior-day tape, check whether the lag was driven by a T-1 catalyst (Fed decision, macro print, single-name shock) that has already had a full session to transmit. If the catalyst is T-1 and the current morning shows continuation-drift (not fresh shock), treat the prior-day tape as potentially exhausted and score S4=0 or require same-morning confirmation of continued downside. Additionally, when the same T-1 event appears in both S0 and S1, apply it once — if the gold slide is the S1 driver, do not also count it in S0 unless there is independent macro transmission (e.g., real yields still rising on the day, not just carryover). When a compositional buffer is identified, consider whether it justifies not just capping magnitude but also questioning the direction's strength — if the buffer nearly offsets the drag (as it did: XLB −0.34% vs SPY −0.39%, rel +0.04%), the score should be closer to flat/flat than down/mild.

## WHEN IT FIRES
Sector ETF prediction where the prior session's decisive relative lag (1d rel ≤ −1.5%) is treated as confirmation of continued downside (S4=−1) rather than as potentially exhausted/priced-in tape, especially when (a) the lag was driven by a T-1 catalyst (gold slide on Fed decision) that has already transmitted through Wednesday's close and Thursday's session, (b) the current morning shows the catalyst is continuation-drift not fresh shock, and (c) the sector has a compositional buffer (chemicals/steel child-books long) that the model identifies but underweights in magnitude calibration — the model scores S4=−1 on the stale tape and double-counts the T-1 gold fade across S0 and S1, producing a −2.7 total that overstates the downside versus an actual near-flat session (XLB −0.34%, rel +0.04%).

## WRONG IF
This lesson would be falsified by a case where the prior session had a decisive relative lag (≤ −1.5%) driven by a T-1 catalyst, the current morning shows continuation-drift only, and the actual session delivers continued decisive downside (rel ≤ −1.5%) — i.e., the stale tape was NOT exhausted and S4=−1 was correct. If such a case occurs, the lesson's assumption that T-1 catalysts fully transmit within one session would be wrong, and the correction would need to distinguish between catalysts that exhaust quickly (gold slides on Fed decisions) versus those that propagate (e.g., a fundamental deterioration in China demand that continues to drag across multiple sessions).

## EVIDENCE
2026-09-04 Basic Materials: Morning predicted down/mild (total −2.7, S4=−1 on 1d rel −1.67% through 09-03). Actual: XLB −0.34%, SPY −0.39%, rel +0.04% — direction correct but magnitude at the very flat end, with the prior-day tape fully mean-reverting. The outcome review explicitly flagged: "S4=−1 was the key error... the morning treated the prior day's decisive lag as confirmation of continued downside, but the tape had already mean-reverted." The gold slide was a Wednesday event (T-1) double-counted across S0 and S1; Friday's gold move was continuation drift. The chemicals/steel buffer held as predicted, nearly offsetting the gold/miner drag entirely.
