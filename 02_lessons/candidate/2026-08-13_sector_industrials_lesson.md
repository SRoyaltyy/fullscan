# Sector Reflection — Industrials — 2026-08-13

LESSON_BEGIN
ERROR_CATEGORY: B
TRIGGER_PATTERN: When an oil-sensitive cyclical sector (Industrials) is being scored while a prior-session geopolitical supply-shock headline (e.g., Hormuz, Brent near $90) is still in the news, but the pre-fetched oil tape is down and fresh demand-side catalysts are available (OPEC/IEA demand-forecast cuts, large inventory builds, official comments on normal flows), the model treats the headline as the current-session truth and discards the tape. The correct behavior is to check whether the demand-side catalyst has already flipped the day’s oil direction; if so, the headline is the stale leg and the sector bias should be down/flat-to-mild, not up/mild.
CURRENT_BEHAVIOR: The model sees a “live” geopolitical oil-supply headline, concludes the pre-fetched CL/BZ down print is stale, caps S0 at 0, leaves structural positives (ISM, AI-power, freight) to create an up bias, and emits up/mild despite the negative 1d XLI relative tape and a valid demand-side oil selloff. It over-applies the prior 08-11/08-12 Industrials lesson by deciding any supply-shock headline automatically overrides the actual oil tape.
CORRECTED_BEHAVIOR: Before discarding a pre-fetched oil direction under an active geopolitical headline, verify whether the oil move is corroborated by a fresh demand-side catalyst (OPEC/IEA demand revision, inventory build, demand-destruction data). If oil is down for demand reasons while the old supply headline remains in the news, treat the headline as prior-session noise for today’s session; set S0 ≤ 0, do not add an up bias from structural factors alone, and with a negative 1d relative tape prefer down/flat-to-mild rather than up/mild.
EVIDENCE: 2026-08-13 XLI fell -0.048% while SPY rose +0.698%, producing -0.746% relative underperformance. The morning prediction said “oil is NOT down” because Bloomberg reported Brent holding just below $89, but actual oil fell ~2% to $87.20–87.69 on OPEC cutting 2026 demand-growth forecasts and a surprise +9.07M barrel API crude build. The pre-fetched oil tape (CL/BZ down) was directionally correct; the “live” headline was the stale prior-session state. Direction miss scored by the scoreboard.
LESSON_MATCH_CHECK: The 2026-08-12 Industrials lesson matched the setup and was applied, but it was too broad: it warned against treating the pre-fetched oil tape as authoritative, yet here the pre-fetched oil tape was right. The 2026-08-13 Energy lesson already identified the correct demand-side negative oil spine but was not transferred to Industrials. No existing exact lesson covers “supply headline still active, demand catalyst has flipped oil’s direction, and the correct sector call is down/flat-to-mild for Industrials.”
BACKWARD_CHECK: This refinement would have helped 08-10 (up/notable vs -0.313) and 08-13 (up/mild vs -0.048) by allowing a down/flat bias when demand-side oil evidence was present. It does not contradict 08-11 (up/severe vs +0.596) because that session did not have the fresh OPEC/API demand-side reversal pattern. It also preserves the 08-12 magnitude-cap lesson rather than replacing it.
CONFLICT_CHECK: This refines, rather than conflicts with, the 08-11/08-12 Industrials lesson. Those lessons rightly say not to treat a pre-fetched oil print as authoritative when the geopolitical headline is two-sided. The refinement is: if a fresh demand-side catalyst corroborates the pre-fetched oil direction, that print is authoritative for the day and the geopolitical headline is the stale leg. This is also consistent with the 08-13 Communication Services lesson: do not double-count stale risk-off signals.
FALSIFIER: This rule would be falsified if a future session has an active Hormuz-type headline, a fresh OPEC/API demand-side selloff signal, pre-fetched oil down, and a negative XLI 1d relative tape, yet XLI closes positive and outperforms SPY. Alternatively, if Brent actually holds its prior level and oil rises, then up/mild would be correct and the “demand-side flip” reading would be wrong.
DIVERGENCE_VERDICT: futures_right
ACTIVE_LESSON_REVIEW: The active 08-11/08-12 Industrials lesson was applied but over-corrected the oil read; it should be amended to require a demand-side catalyst check before labeling the pre-fetched oil tape stale. The 08-13 Energy lesson is consistent and worth generalizing to other oil-sensitive sectors. The scoreboard miss is not an accounting error; it is a reasoning/over-correction failure.
SECTOR: Industrials
LESSON_END
