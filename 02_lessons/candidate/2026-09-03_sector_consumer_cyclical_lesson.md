# Sector Reflection — Consumer Cyclical — 2026-09-03

LESSON_BEGIN
ERROR_CATEGORY: A
TRIGGER_PATTERN: A sector prediction treats a live two-sided Fed narrative (hike vs hold odds near a coin flip) as a fully "carried" S0=0 overlay based on prior sessions having "already paid" the hawkish side, when a voting Fed member has a same-calendar-day appearance (speech/interview/Q&A) that could deliver a fresh dovish or hawkish counter-signal. The morning's flat-futures snapshot (ES within ±0.5%) is then used as confirmation that no fresh catalyst is pending, when in fact the snapshot predates the scheduled appearance window.
CURRENT_BEHAVIOR: When a Fed narrative (e.g., Warsh September-hike odds) has been live for 2+ sessions and prior predictions already scored it as a headwind, the model marks it "carried" and sets S0=0, explicitly refusing to re-score it. The model checks only the next *formal* FOMC date or the next *scheduled* speech date in Channel 2, but does not check for same-day unscheduled/semi-scheduled appearances (Reuters Q&A, press avail, live blog) by voting members. A flat futures snapshot in the morning data window is treated as evidence that "nothing is breaking today," even when the snapshot is taken before the appearance window.
CORRECTED_BEHAVIOR: When a Fed narrative is live and two-sided (hike odds in the 40–70% range, not a settled consensus), the model must check the economic/political calendar for ANY same-calendar-day appearance by a voting Fed member (Chair, Governor, or regional President with a vote) — including unscheduled Q&A, press gaggles, interviews, or newsmaker events — not just formal speeches. If such an appearance exists and the morning snapshot predates it, the "carried" treatment is INVALID: the S0=0 must be replaced with a conditional S0 that acknowledges a fresh catalyst is pending. Specifically: (a) do not use a flat futures snapshot taken before the appearance window as evidence that no catalyst is pending; (b) if the Fed narrative is two-sided and a voting member speaks same-day, treat S0 as a binary risk (either −1 or +1 depending on the member's known lean) rather than a settled 0; (c) when the member's known lean is dovish (e.g., Waller has historically favored disinflation patience), the risk skew is asymmetric — a dovish surprise is more likely to move markets than a re-statement of the hawkish status quo, so the S0 should carry a dovish-tilted uncertainty rather than a neutral 0.
