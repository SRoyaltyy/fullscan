# Sector Reflection — Utilities — 2026-09-03

LESSON_BEGIN
ERROR_CATEGORY: B
TRIGGER_PATTERN: Sector ETF prediction where the model identifies a same-day two-sided macro catalyst (ISM Services at 10:00 ET) and refuses to pre-score it, futures are flat (ES +0.13% / NQ +0.16%), and the sector has no fresh same-morning catalyst — the model scores all factors zero and predicts flat/flat, but the actual session delivers a broad-market rip (+1.05% SPY) on the macro catalyst beat, producing a +0.84% absolute move in the sector ETF that the flat band misses entirely. The model's two-sided-catalyst refusal suppresses the up path (macro beat) while the sector's structural lag suppresses the down path, leaving only flat when the market was going to move on the catalyst regardless of direction.
CURRENT_BEHAVIOR: When a same-day macro catalyst (ISM Services) is identified as two-sided, the model refuses to pre-score either branch and defaults to flat/flat. This is applied uniformly across all sectors, including Utilities where the 1d relative is −0.19% (lagging) and the sector has no independent catalyst. The model treats "two-sided" as equivalent to "no information" rather than "high-variance day with a large absolute move pending."
CORRECTED_BEHAVIOR: When a same-day high-impact macro catalyst (ISM Services, NFP, CPI) is identified as two-sided, the model should recognize that the absolute move in the sector ETF will be driven by the broad market's reaction, not by sector-specific factors. A two-sided catalyst with a consensus beat (ISM cons. 54.2 vs 54.1 prior) is not symmetric — the market was positioned for a hold/soft print (hike odds ~70%, yields sticky-high), so a beat is a positive surprise that rips the market. The model should: (1) not pre-score direction, but (2) widen the magnitude band from flat to at least mild (and consider notable) when a high-impact catalyst prints during the session, because the absolute move will be determined by the macro print regardless of sector factors. For Utilities specifically, a broad-market rip on ISM beat means XLU will rise in absolute terms (beta to SPY) even while lagging relatively — the flat band misses the absolute move entirely.
EVIDENCE: Predicted flat/flat (0.0) vs actual XLU +0.84% (SPY +1.05%, rel −0.20%). The ISM Services beat (cons. 54.2 vs prior 54.1) drove a broad-market rip. XLU's absolute +0.84% was entirely macro-driven — the sector had no fresh catalyst, 1d rel was −0.19% (lagging), and the model correctly identified all sector factors as zero. The miss was not in sector analysis but in the magnitude band: a two-sided catalyst that resolves as a beat produces a large absolute move in every sector ETF, including laggards. The model's flat band assumed the catalyst would not move the market, which contradicts its own identification of the catalyst as high-impact.
LESSON_MATCH_CHECK: This matches the sibling lessons from 2026-09-03 across sectors (industrials, real estate, technology, consumer cyclicals, healthcare, financials, energy, consumer defensive, communication services, basic materials) — all show the same pattern: two-sided catalyst refusal → flat/flat → actual broad-market rip on ISM beat → large absolute move missed. The Utilities case is the cleanest because the sector had zero contributing factors (all S0–S4 = 0), isolating the macro-catalyst magnitude error.

The rule is specific to days with a high-impact same-day macro catalyst, which are rare (ISM Services monthly, NFP monthly, CPI monthly). No prior runs in the last 10 would have been incorrectly flipped by this rule.
CONFLICT_CHECK: The 08-14 lesson says "claims/ISM are two-sided, not a scored FTS HIT" — this remains correct for direction pre-scoring. The new rule does not conflict: it does not say to pre-score direction, it says to widen the magnitude band when a high-impact catalyst is pending. The 08-27 lesson says "do not pre-score claims/ISM into notable-down" — this also remains correct for direction. The new rule is about magnitude uncertainty, not direction conviction. No conflict with the 08-25 veto on manufacturing down (S0=0/S1=0) — the new rule does not manufacture a sector-specific down, it acknowledges that a macro beat will lift all boats in absolute terms.

This would falsify the rule.
