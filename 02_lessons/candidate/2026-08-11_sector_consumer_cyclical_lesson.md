# Sector Reflection — Consumer Cyclical — 2026-08-11

LESSON_BEGIN
ERROR_CATEGORY: A
TRIGGER_PATTERN: A Consumer Cyclical sector call builds a positive S1 score from prior-period consumer-fundamental data (falling gasoline, resilient labor, strong travel/RevPAR) while a fresh knowable-at-open macro shock is active — rising oil/energy prices on geopolitical headlines (e.g., Iran/Hormuz) and/or a scheduled high-impact macro print (CPI) looming. The morning treats the live energy-cost factor as a stale “MISS/inverted” from prior-month data and labels the tape “converging” even though futures are flat-to-negative and XLY’s mega-cap concentration has not confirmed strength.
CURRENT_BEHAVIOR: On 2026-08-11, Consumer Cyclical was scored up/mild with S1 = +1 because July gasoline prices had fallen, jobless claims were 199K, and hotel RevPAR forecasts were raised. The live premarket oil spike (WTI +1.89% to $83.68, Brent +1.68% to $89.19 on Iran/Hormuz risk) and pre-CPI caution were not weighted in S1. The divergence note said “CONVERGING,” and the deterministic score emitted up/mild. Actual: XLY -0.36%, SPY -0.32%, REL -0.04% — a down/flat day.
CORRECTED_BEHAVIOR: Before scoring S1 for Consumer Cyclical, check current-day energy prices and headlines (WTI, Brent, gasoline/RBOB futures, Strait of Hormuz/Iran conflict) rather than relying on a prior-month gasoline price series. If oil is rising on a geopolitical shock and/or a high-impact CPI/NFP/FOMC print is imminent, treat the “gasoline spike crushing discretionary” factor as a live negative (HIT), not a stale inverted MISS. Down-weight prior-month fundamental positives (labor, travel, July gas) when the live tape is risk-off. Set S1 ≤ 0, avoid an up call when broad futures are flat-to-negative, and only call fundamentals and tape “converging” if the 1d/3d tape is actually confirming the bullish fundamentals.
EVIDENCE: Prediction: up/mild, total_score 3.0, divergence_flagged False. Actual: XLY -0.36%, SPY -0.32%, REL -0.04%. Oil rose sharply on Iran/Hormuz conflict; CPI was due next morning; The Financial Spectator noted “Technology Rallies, but XLY and XLC Fail to Confirm.” The morning’s gasoline factor was derived from TradingEconomics July data, not the live Aug 11 oil move. Had S1 been corrected from +1 to -1, the output would have flipped from up/mild to down/flat, making the direction hit.
